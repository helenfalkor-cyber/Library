Section 2
Spot the Contradiction
Each card below shows a real public statement from a major fashion brand alongside a documented fact that contradicts it. For each pair: identify exactly what is contradicted, and write one sentence explaining how the brand is misrepresenting reality.
Brand A — H&M
What the brand says
"We are committed to making fashion sustainable and sustainability fashionable. Our goal is to use only recycled or sustainably sourced materials by 2030."
What the record shows
H&M produces approximately 3 billion garments per year. Its in-store recycling bins collect donated items, but customers who donate receive a coupon — incentivising them to buy more. Environmental scorecards on H&M's website have been found to include data described as "the exact opposite of reality."
What exactly is being contradicted — and how?
Brand B — Zara
What the brand says
"We have a zero tolerance policy towards forced labour. We are committed to due diligence across our global supply chains."
What the record shows
In 2011, Brazil's government filed 52 charges against Zara's parent company Inditex after inspectors rescued workers from a São Paulo factory operating under slave-like conditions — one worker was fourteen years old.
What exactly is being contradicted — and how?
Brand C — Zara
What the brand says
"Our Join Life collection represents our move towards a circular economy model and sustainable materials — extending the life cycle of our products."
What the record shows
The Join Life label accounts for less than 5% of Zara's total production volume. Zara releases approximately 20 collections per year. Less than 1% of items dropped into its recycling bins is ever recycled into new fabric.
What exactly is being contradicted — and how?
Section 3
Cleft Sentences
Cleft sentences split one idea into two clauses to place emphasis on a specific element.
It-cleft: It is the consumer who drives demand.
Wh-cleft: What shocks most people is the scale of the waste.
Build a cleft sentence that emphasises the focus element shown.

Section 4
Discourse Markers for Argument
Nevertheless / Nonetheless — contrast after conceding a point
In contrast — direct opposition between two ideas
By the same token — applying the same logic to a new point
As a consequence — result of what was just stated
What is more — adding a stronger or more surprising point
It could be argued that — introducing a position with some distance
