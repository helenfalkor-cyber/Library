Section 4
Mixed Conditionals
Mixed conditionals combine time frames — a past condition with a present result, or a present condition with a past result.

Type 1 — Past condition → Present result:
If he had built his empire differently, he wouldn't be so exposed to scrutiny now.
Structure: If + past perfect → would + infinitive

Type 2 — Present condition → Past result:
If she weren't so ruthless, she would never have reached this position.
Structure: If + past simple → would have + past participle
Complete each sentence in your own words. There is no single correct answer — focus on the structure.
01
He never acknowledged the workers' conditions publicly...
Past condition → present result  ·  If he had acknowledged...
02
She is completely indifferent to public opinion...
Present condition → past result  ·  If she weren't...
03
The company chose opacity over transparency from the beginning...
Past condition → present result  ·  If they had chosen...

Give Advice
Read each situation. Give advice or suggest a solution using a mixed conditional and at least one word from the interview vocabulary.
01
Your friend has just returned from a solo trip abroad. She booked everything through a small agency she found online, paid in full upfront, and didn't take out travel insurance. The agency is now unreachable.
What would you tell her now — and what should she have done differently?
02
A fashion blogger you follow has been promoting a brand for months with complete deference — never questioning their opacity about production. She's just found out the brand acts with impunity on labour conditions. She feels embarrassed and doesn't know what to say to her audience.
Give her advice — and reflect on what a different approach would have meant for her now.
03
A friend is considering therapy but keeps dismissing it in a slovenly way — "I'll be fine", "it's not for people like me." She's been saying this for two years.
What would you say to her — and how might things be different if she had started sooner?
Section 5
The Boardroom
Prepare your lines before speaking. This is a confrontational interview — both sides have something to protect.
A major fashion house has just been exposed for paying garment workers $80/month while its CEO took home a €12 million remuneration package last year. You are in a live television interview. The camera is on.
Role A — The Journalist
You have the documents. You have the figures. Your job is to get a real answer — not a framework. Push back on every deflection. Don't let evasion go unchallenged.
Role B — The CEO
You are not lying — you are managing perception. Use the vocabulary from today. Be polished. Be cold. Never lose composure. Never give a direct number.
Prepare — Journalist's opening question
Write the sharpest possible opening question. It should be impossible to deflect cleanly.
Prepare — CEO's opening response
Write a response that acknowledges the question without answering it. Use at least one word from today's vocabulary.
After the role play — reflection
Which role was harder to play — and why? Write 2–3 sentences using a mixed conditional somewhere in your answer.
